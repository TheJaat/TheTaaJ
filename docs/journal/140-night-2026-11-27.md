## What stopped, and the fixes

1. **The first merge: ` M toolchain`.** `one-merge-commit` passed; `merge-item.py` refused - the night tree not clean. Its `toolchain/i686` is a link to the machine's cross compiler, and `toolchain`'s `.gitignore` names a directory; `new-session.sh` hides the link with the submodule's `info/exclude`, the runner's own tree never did. The rehearsal's sandbox has no cross compiler, so no link, and could not see it. The runner had no way to resume, so the eight after it were "not run". **Fixed** in `tree()` (entry 139's branch), and the tested `one-merge-commit` merged by hand with `merge-item.py`, as the runner would have - `main` had not moved since its test, so what merged was exactly what passed. The rest resumed from `docs-split` with a queue without it, rehearsed again first (8 of 8).
2. **The last merge: a crash.** `merge-tools` passed; the runner's `git switch -C merge-tools` failed - the branch was checked out in the tree the runner was started from (mine), and git checks a branch out in one tree only. The error was not caught: the runner stopped with a traceback. **Fixed** here: `merge_now()` catches it and reports the branch "not merged", with why; and `--merge` now refuses to start while any queued branch is checked out in another tree, naming the tree - found in a second, not after an hour of tests. `merge-tools` was merged by hand, as the first was (`main` unchanged since its test).

Checked: the start check, with this branch checked out in my tree and queued - `night-test: --merge, but these queued branches are checked out in other trees: night-fixes: .../TheTaaJ-ci`, exit 1. `merge_now()` with `git switch` failing that way (git stubbed) - `(False, "git switch ... already used by worktree ...")`, no exception.

## For the morning: what could be faster

From the logs' stamps (each part's output comes when it ends):

1. **The suite waits; it is not short of CPUs.** In `one-merge-commit`'s run the shared builds and `smp64-test` were done at 1:29; the parts ran from 1:29 to 10:08 - 8½ minutes - with **3.0 of 24 CPUs busy on average** (30 min of CPU in 10). The last to finish: `aarch64-keys-test` 10:08, `times64-test` 9:56, `schedbench64-test` 9:42, `aarch64-times-test` 9:31, `e1000-64-test` 9:11. Two cheap things to try: **the longest parts first** in `TEST64_PARTS` (make starts the parts in the list's order; the long ones started late finish last), and **more parts at once** than `TEST_JOBS=12` (the machine is mostly idle - but measure the timing tests under the load first, rule 13). Either could take minutes off every run; nine a night.
2. **A minute between tests**: `merge-item.py`, a fetch of both repositories, the checkout, `merge-main.py`, the submodules. Nine branches: 8 to 9 minutes a night. One fetch per branch instead of three or four would cut some.
3. **Skipping parts whose inputs did not change** (left for later by the user): every one of tonight's branches but `j96-coverage` and `test64-no-rebuild` changed only tools and documents; most of their 9½ minutes ran the same images again.
4. **The rehearsal could not see the tree's cleanliness.** Its sandbox should have a `toolchain/i686` link like the real trees.
5. **A resume.** Starting again after a stop took a hand-made queue; `--from BRANCH` would do it.
